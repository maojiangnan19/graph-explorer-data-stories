01 / Starting point

Our question

If we remove a character from the Marvel network, which choice makes the surviving network fall apart fastest? A character with many direct links is an obvious candidate. But a bridge between regions may matter for a different reason.

    04 / What the game reveals

    High degree is only one hypothesis

    A hub can have many neighbors while its surrounding region stays well connected through other paths. A broker can have fewer links but sit on many shortest paths between regions. The experiment gives those competing intuitions the same network and the same removal budget.

    DegreeHow many direct connections?
    ClosenessHow close am I to everyone else?
    BetweennessHow often am I on shortest paths?
    PageRank / EigenvectorAre my neighbors important?

    06 / Beyond one node

    Networks also have group structure

    Assortativity

    Do high-degree nodes connect to high-degree nodes? This measures whether similar degrees tend to meet.

    Homophily

    Do similar nodes connect to similar nodes? Similarity might mean role, type, or another attribute.

    Clique

    Is everyone in a group connected to everyone else? A group of k nodes contains E = k(k − 1) / 2 possible pairwise links.

    07 / Takeaway

    There is no single “most important” node

    Different centrality measures answer different questions. If the question is “which removals are most likely to fragment the network?”, betweenness is a useful structural measure to compare, not an automatic winner.

    The next question is always: compared to what? A high centrality score may partly follow from a node’s degree or from the network’s overall shape.

    08 / Methods

    How this experiment works

    Data and calculations

    We load the 303 nodes and 1,784 directed links from the Week 1 course snapshot. For the removal game, reciprocal and one-way links are projected into a simple undirected adjacency list. After each removal, breadth-first search finds connected components and the largest component becomes the “Core”. Degree, closeness, and betweenness are calculated on the surviving undirected graph; PageRank is calculated on the original directed links with a damping factor of 0.85. The random baseline averages 20 seeded runs per comparison.
